# The Visitor — script (fictional example)

约 6 镜 / one scene / two characters. Written to exercise the workflow, not to be moving.

## Characters

- **The Father** — 52. Runs a small haulage business. Has not seen his son in eleven years.
  Speaks little; when he does, it is a transaction.
- **The Son** — 19. Grew up in a rented room with a man who was paid to keep him.
  Keeps a canvas bag that holds everything he owns.

## Scene 1 — The flat · night · interior

The flat is small and kept. A sofa with a worn cover, a coffee table, a television that is on
with the sound down. Rain on the window.

The door opens. The Son stands in the doorway, soaked through, a canvas bag over one shoulder.
He does not come in.

THE FATHER (from the sofa, not looking up): "You're wet."

THE SON: "It's raining."

THE FATHER: "Sit down."

The Son looks at the sofa, then at the floor. He steps in, but he does not sit. He stands with
the bag still on his shoulder, dripping onto the floorboards.

THE FATHER: "I'll get a towel."

He gets up. The Son watches him go, and for the first time his face does something — not relief,
not anger. Something closer to calculation: *how much of this is real.*

The Father returns with a towel. He holds it out. Neither of them moves to close the distance.

THE FATHER: "Take it."

The Son takes it. He does not dry his hair. He holds the towel in both hands and looks at the
wet floorboards, at the two of them standing on either side of them.
